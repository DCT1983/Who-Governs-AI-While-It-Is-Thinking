# Who Governs AI While It Is Thinking?

## Comparative Runtime Governance and the Economic Wall of Accuracy

**Author:** Darren Tindale
**Date:** September 2026
**Status:** Manuscript submitted to *AI and Ethics* (Springer Nature) for consideration

## Overview

This paper examines a gap in contemporary AI governance: how an AI system should be governed **while computation is still occurring**, before an action becomes irreversible.

It proposes a comparative runtime-governance architecture built around:

* an always-operative **Reference (baseline) pathway**;
* one or more conditional **Higher-fidelity pathways**;
* persistent runtime state;
* comparison between candidate outputs or system states;
* cumulative resource and governance constraints;
* runtime decisions to permit, suppress, pause, terminate, or reopen higher-fidelity processing; and
* explicit selection of the candidate or disposition that determines the final outcome.

The paper also introduces the **Economic Wall of Accuracy**: the point at which the expected marginal benefit of further accuracy-improving computation no longer justifies its marginal computational, financial, latency, or operational cost for the decision at hand.
